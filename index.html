MY STORY

The path makes sense when you see the whole thing.

I did not begin by trying to become an “AI person.” Each step came from working closer to real systems, real constraints and real operational problems.

2010–2015

Engineering in Japan

I studied Mechanical Engineering at Meiji University in Tokyo under a Malaysian-Japanese government scholarship. The entire degree was conducted in Japanese, and I graduated with a CGPA of 3.71/4.00.

My thesis already showed the pattern I would keep repeating later: use whatever tools are available to solve the real problem. I built a Raspberry Pi and Python-based vehicle dynamics simulator with servo actuation and data logging.

2015–2018

Systems thinking through engineering, CAE and motorsport

I worked in engineering and CAE environments, including motorsport-related work. I learned to think in systems, trade-offs, constraints and failure modes rather than isolated tasks. One example was using a design-of-experiments approach in MSC Adams instead of brute-forcing simulation runs.

I also spent time in Japan-market application quality and insights work, which strengthened the bridge between technical detail, user experience and Japanese-language business context.

2018–2025

Learning regulated operations from the inside

I moved into a global financial-services environment and spent years in high-volume back-office operations. This is where I learned what “enterprise reality” actually looks like: legacy systems, strict controls, auditability, repetitive work, exceptions, handoffs and the cost of small process inefficiencies.

Across multi-year operational work, I maintained 99%+ accuracy while handling card-member data maintenance, credit write-off processing and related controlled workflows.

2023–Now

Building a parallel AI and automation lane

Outside my core role, I began experimenting more seriously with AI, automation and workflow design. I also contributed as an independent consultant to a US-based AI startup on marketing positioning and AI-video strategy.

A lot of this work happened under awkward constraints — limited time, limited budget and often only an iPhone during the day. That constraint shaped my approach: keep systems simple, modular, documented and easy to continue across sessions and tools.

2025–Now

Japan merchant settlement and payment operations

My current operational focus is Japan-market merchant settlement and payment processing. I work with multi-processor routing and reconciliation, raw IBM mainframe / PCOM settlement data, settlement timing differences, pay-plan changes, merchant lifecycle data and exception handling.

This gave me something I now see as valuable for AI transformation work: domain depth. I understand not only the technology idea, but the messy operational boundary where data, controls, regulation, legacy systems and human judgment meet.

2026 →

From operations to transformation and solution design

The direction is now deliberate: financial-services operations → transformation → AI & automation transformation → business solution design → regional/international solution architecture.

I am not trying to leave operations behind. I am using it as the domain foundation for designing better systems.

ENTERPRISE AI / RAG

Mapping ~200 SOPs without dumping everything into the model

When a large document dump produced unreliable answers, I diagnosed the retrieval problem and designed a two-step approach: first identify the relevant document titles deterministically, then query only the correct source documents. The goal was simple: reduce hallucination and make SOP retrieval usable under real enterprise constraints.

Production-used workflow · hierarchical retrieval pattern

HOW I WORK

The principles behind the projects.

Start with the operational problem.

I care about what creates delay, risk, rework or confusion before I care about which tool is fashionable.

Separate reasoning from execution.

AI can interpret messy intent. Deterministic controls should own permission, validation, approval and execution where risk matters.

Find the requirement beneath the process.

A manual workflow is often only the visible shell. I look for the underlying data, rule, decision, action and evidence.

Design for constraints.

Legacy systems, limited permissions, iPhone-only access, small budgets and enterprise policies are design inputs — not excuses.

Keep claims evidence-based.

I distinguish production results, enterprise testing and portfolio prototypes. I would rather understate than pretend.

Translate between business and technical people.

My value is often in connecting operational pain, controls, data and technical possibilities into something people can actually implement.
